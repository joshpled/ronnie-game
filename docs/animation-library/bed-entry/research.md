# Motion references for Ronnie’s bed entry

## Sources and observations

- [Vetstreet — Dr. Sarah Wooten, “Why Does My Dog… Walk in a Circle Before Lying Down?”](https://www.vetstreet.com/our-pet-experts/video-why-does-my-dog-walk-in-a-circle-before-lying-down), [YouTube version](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rAy9ZVnxdm4). The publisher links a downloadable clip. I inspected extracted frames at quarter-second intervals. Around 35–37 s, the small cream dog lowers its nose nearly to the floor. Around 37–38 s, shoulders turn and the hindquarters follow through a compact partial arc. Around 38–41 s, it makes small paw adjustments while investigating the spot. Around 41–42 s, its front lowers before the hindquarters settle. These are observed actions in this individual clip, not a universal sequence or measured joint trajectory.
- [Life with Malamutes — “PHILosophy 101”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g-SaZq41UyM). Automated scene analysis places repeated circling/digging at roughly 15–56 s and lying down after that. I opened the footage in the browser, but did not measure its feet frame by frame. It supports the broader interrupted nesting pattern only; it is not a timing or anatomy authority for little Ronnie. The dog is also being encouraged, so its energetic repetition is a poor pacing match for our calm sequence.
- [University of Minnesota veterinary anatomy — Normal Walk](https://vanat.ahc.umn.edu/gaits/walk.html). Its eight-frame teaching animation provides clear support and swing poses. Walking alternates two- and three-limb support; each foot spends more time supporting than swinging. I extracted its eight actual frames as motion-only guidance. The head, breed, coat and diagram style are not Ronnie references.

All source clips/frame guides stay in the private working directory, outside the game repository. Public deliverables contain source links and Ronnie’s generated artwork, not copied footage. The original atlas and original photos remain the character/style authorities. Reference dogs are used only for motion.

## Correction to automated analysis

The automated Vetstreet summary claimed the black/tan dog circles twice at 21–30 s. Direct frame inspection disproved that: the dog is already lying down and later lowers its head. That claim is excluded. The small cream dog’s actual 35–42 s action is the relevant inspected segment. Automated scene descriptions are discovery aids, not footfall evidence.

## Changes to attempt

1. Lower Ronnie’s nose toward the cushion and curve the body into the turn, with head/shoulders initiating and the hips following.
2. Replace the constant-rate circular path with a tighter, slightly uneven arc and brief investigation holds. Exact timings and the full turn remain animation choices, not a trace of measured source coordinates.
3. Use the veterinary eight-phase reference for the approach’s distinct support/lift/swing/plant poses. A permanently trailing hind foot fails this criterion.
4. Keep body scale stable across view changes and shorten the stride. Preserve the original rendering, face, black neck, tan head, saddle and speckled paws.
5. Keep the approved bed separate and preserve the exact approved curled destination. The lie-down transition remains the next user checkpoint, not part of this revision.

## Limits of copying from these references

The inspected small-dog clip shows a partial turn plus repositioning, not a clean isolated full revolution. Its long fur hides joints. This revision adapts its visible motion pattern; it does not claim motion capture, exact foot-contact reconstruction or frame-perfect copying. Generated artwork and the final playback still need their own visual review.

## Result of this attempt

The low-headed circle family was selected for review, with a tighter arc and two 320 ms investigation pauses. The three approach attempts did not meet both gait and identity requirements, so playback retains the preceding approach. Teaching-diagram pose transfer improved alternating legs but changed head/leg proportions; the proportion repair lost the rear-leg alternation. The revision therefore improves nesting posture and pacing without claiming a complete natural walk. Foot sliding, repeated headings and the approach-to-low-head change remain visible.
